Quantum Foundations & Generalized Noncontextuality

MS Thesis Project · Supervised by Dr. Beata Zjawin (ICTQT, Poland)

For my master's thesis, I investigate the boundaries of genuine quantum nonclassicality through the framework of generalized noncontextuality. Rather than interpreting standard Hilbert space formalism, this approach relies on Leibniz's principle of the identity of indiscernibles. By demanding that operationally equivalent preparation and measurement procedures correspond to identical representations in any underlying ontological model, we can rigorously isolate the "thin film" of genuine quantum novelty from phenomena that are fully classically explainable.

My core research focuses on the typicality of contextuality: determining how often randomly chosen quantum preparations and measurements produce statistics that resist classical explanation. By employing numerical linear programming to test whether the generalized probabilistic theory (GPT) fragments associated with these experiments admit of a simplex embedding, we can systematically certify nonclassicality. Our findings demonstrate that contextuality is surprisingly ubiquitous; even in scenarios with a modest number of random preparations and measurements, nonclassical statistics arise with over 99% probability.

Crucially, I explore how this typicality persists under realistic experimental limitations, such as decreasing the purity of preparations or relying on unsharp measurements (POVMs). Beyond fundamental theory, we also analyze the practical utility of these nonclassical resources in information processing tasks like parity-oblivious multiplexing (POM). This work reveals that while stumbling upon contextuality is highly typical, achieving a quantitatively large quantum advantage requires more stringent experimental alignment and control.
