Research Interests

My research journey explores the fundamental physical frameworks of our universe—spanning from the macroscopic evolution of the early cosmos to the deep, structural rules of quantum mechanics. I am deeply interested in theoretical models that bridge abstract mathematical concepts with the physical realities they describe.

"I am driven by a desire to understand the foundational rules of nature, whether they dictate the origins of cosmic structures or the non-classical behavior of quantum information."

Current & Past Projects

Quantum Foundations & Causal Inference

MS Thesis Project · Supervised by Dr. Beata Zjawin (ICTQT, Poland)

For my master's thesis, I am transitioning into the field of quantum foundations. Working remotely with the International Centre for Theory of Quantum Technologies (ICTQT), we are exploring the theoretical underpinnings of quantum mechanics. My current focus centers on the themes of quantum contextuality and causal inference. While the specific boundaries of the research problem are currently taking shape, the overarching goal is to investigate the non-classical structures that dictate quantum correlations and understand how they fundamentally differ from classical causal models.


Cosmic Magnetic Fields & Dark Matter

Undergraduate Research · Supervised by Prof. Koushik Dutta (IISER Kolkata)

Prior to my thesis work, I focused on early universe cosmology. Specifically, our work investigated the generation of primordial cosmic magnetic fields. We explored a recently proposed magnetogenesis mechanism driven by the dynamics of ultralight dark matter. This project involved extensive literature review and theoretical study, focusing on how scalar fields and dark matter couplings in the early universe could seed the vast magnetic fields we observe in the cosmos today.
