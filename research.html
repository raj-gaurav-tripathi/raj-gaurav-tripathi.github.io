Main Research Directions and Current Projects

My work explores the practical foundations of trustworthy computation: how complex systems can be studied, communicated, and improved without losing sight of the people who use them.

“The most useful technical work is not only correct; it is also clear enough to be questioned, adapted, and shared.”

Our research combines formal methods, numerical experiments, and collaborative software practice. The projects below are intentionally written as editable placeholder content. Replace their titles, descriptions, and links with your own research directions.

  1. Reliable computational models. We develop methods for testing assumptions, estimating uncertainty, and communicating limitations in systems that inform real decisions. Read a sample project.
  2. Human-centred technical tools. We study interfaces and workflows that help expert and non-expert users understand complex analysis without oversimplifying it.
  3. Open and reproducible research. We create lightweight processes for sharing code, data, and explanations so that results can be independently examined and reused.
