Research Interests

My research journey explores the fundamental physical frameworks of our universe—spanning from the macroscopic evolution of the early cosmos to the deep, structural rules of quantum mechanics. I am deeply interested in theoretical models that bridge abstract mathematical concepts with the physical realities they describe.

"I am driven by a desire to understand the foundational rules of nature, whether they dictate the origins of cosmic structures or the non-classical behavior of quantum information."

Current & Past Projects

Quantum Foundations & Causal Inference

MS Thesis Project · Supervised by Dr. Beata Zjawin (ICTQT, Poland)

For my master's thesis, I am working in the field of quantum foundations, specifically investigating the operational framework of generalized noncontextuality and causal inference. Rather than interpreting standard Hilbert space formalism, we analyze the statistics of prepare-and-measure scenarios to identify genuine non-classicality. By demanding that operationally equivalent preparation procedures ($\rho_P = \rho_{P'}$) correspond to identical probability distributions over hidden variables in an underlying ontological model, we can derive robust noncontextuality inequalities. My current focus centers on exploring the typicality of contextuality using mixed states and unsharp measurements (POVMs), moving beyond the idealized projective constraints of the Kochen-Specker theorem. Ultimately, we aim to understand how the non-classical structures dictating quantum correlations fundamentally deviate from classical causal models and Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs).


Cosmic Magnetic Fields & Dark Matter

Undergraduate Research · Supervised by Prof. Koushik Dutta (IISER Kolkata)

Prior to my thesis, I focused on early universe cosmology and late-time magnetogenesis driven by ultralight dark matter (ULDM). We investigated how a coherently oscillating pseudo-scalar dark matter condensate $\phi(t)$ couples to the electromagnetic field via a parity-violating Chern-Simons term $\frac{1}{4} g_{\phi\gamma} \phi F_{\mu\nu} \tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}$. This time-dependent background modifies the photon dispersion relation, driving a tachyonic instability for modes $k < g_{\phi\gamma} m_\phi \Phi a(\eta)$ and inducing exponential amplification of the gauge field. Our theoretical analysis evaluated backreaction constraints and resonance conditions using Floquet exponents derived from the Mathieu equation. Specifically, we mathematically examined whether the finite electrical conductivity ($\sigma$) of the primordial plasma overdamps the resonance, and explored mechanisms like inverse magnetohydrodynamic cascades and supercooled trapped misalignment to preserve astrophysically relevant magnetic fields on megaparsec scales.
